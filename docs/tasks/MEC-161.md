# MEC-161 — integrated pitch output fork prototype

Completed CAD prototype, 2026-10-09. Each upper/lower output combines its existing
front plate and rear bearing arm with a 12mm wide, 6mm thick side web. Existing
horn slots, bearing seat, retainer fixings, saddle/foot interfaces and70/85mm
link geometry are retained. The separate bridge and its two long bolts disappear.

Source: `mechanical/prototype-pitch-fork.scad`; local assembly viewer:
`mechanical/prototype-pitch-fork-assembly.scad` and its PNG. Four experimental
STLs provide both hands of both forks. These are working CAD, not released parts.
The supported bench-3 manufacturing kit is unchanged.

## Independent checks

`node mechanical/render-pitch-fork-checks.mjs` exports opposite hands and checks
CGAL intersections at upper20/44.0486/55deg and lower60/78.9786/90deg. All six
intersections with modeled cases/supports are empty. This is sampled local pitch
clearance, not a complete leg sweep or confirmation of actual ears/leads/horns.

`node mechanical/check-pitch-fork.mjs` checks closed connected STL surfaces,
bed origin, printer bounds, opposite-hand point correspondence at0.001mm and
actual mesh rays through horn, bearing, retainer and saddle/foot interfaces.
Upper bounds118.574 x78.2 x32mm; lower96 x78.2 x32mm. All checks pass.
The local viewer imports the original prints using the inverse proper print
rotation; its rendered assembly was visually inspected. Existing reflected
pitch frames require opposite physical prints; whole-leg integration is deferred.

## Assembly and printing

Print side-on in the supplied STL orientation: the long web sits on the bed.
Use a brim if needed and inspect the slicer for local supports beneath the
round plates and horizontal bearing/horn bores. Do not assume support-free
printing or that the old flat-printed bearing coupon qualifies this orientation.
Keep supports removable from interfaces. Insert bearings from the outside,
fit separate retainers, then attach the horn and existing saddle/foot carrier.
Unused former bridge holes may remain; no bolts go in those positions.

The side web is a conservative prototype geometry, not a tested load rating.
Print one pair, check hole/seat fit and assembly access, then gradually load it
before committing to eight forks. No structural optimization/FEA was performed.
